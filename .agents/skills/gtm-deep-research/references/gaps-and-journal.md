# Research gaps, evidence exhaustion and investigation journal

[Book home](../book/index.md) · [Chapter 03](../book/part-02-investigation/03-research-contracts-and-investigation.md) · [Investigation workflow](investigation-execution.md)

## 1. Identify consequential gaps

Maintain a project-scoped `GAP-...` record for a meaningful unresolved question: missing/contradictory evidence, inaccessible original record, ambiguous GTM definition, unknown timing, unavailable first-customer proof, or unanswered causal mechanism. Link each gap to its contract question, affected claims/concepts and relevant sources. Do not require a file per minor gap.

A gap's **criticality is impact-based**, judged against:

- mandatory charter question or approved completion criterion;
- importance to a major historical or causal conclusion;
- downstream evidence or plan dependencies;
- strategic interpretation and transferable lessons;
- evidence integrity, independence and contradictions;
- scope/assumptions and risk of misrepresenting the answer.

Criticality is not an estimate of search difficulty, money or inconvenience. Do not downgrade a gap because it is expensive or awkward to research. Record the assessment and rationale; reassess if impact changes.

## 2. Exhaustion vs. blocked access vs. budget exhaustion

A lead may be **evidence-exhausted** only after documenting reasonable distinct authorized avenues and why additional available work is unlikely to materially improve the answer. A single failed search, a few agreeing articles, an inaccessible original, or reached budget does **not** establish exhaustion.

Distinguish:

- `open` — useful evidence leads still exist;
- `blocked` — inaccessible source, needed authorization, unsafe budget or dependency;
- `exhausted-noncritical` — justified limited closure, with explicit unknowns and revisit triggers;
- `escalated-critical` — unresolved consequential issue requiring decision/qualified disposition;
- `resolved` — satisfactory evidence addresses the question.

These are **gap statuses** (not the claim's evidence state, review state or execution state).

## 3. Autonomous closure of noncritical gaps (DR-012)

Codex may close a noncritical, adequately exhausted gap **without human approval** if the closure record states:

1. Exact unresolved question and contract linkage.
2. Why it matters and a justified **noncritical** assessment.
3. Investigated sources and distinct avenues, including failed attempts and legitimate recovery paths.
4. What remains unknown and the narrowest answer the evidence supports.
5. Why further authorized investigation is unlikely to materially help now.
6. Effect on downstream claims/concepts, hypotheses and conclusions.
7. Explicit **revisit triggers** (e.g. new archive/source, contradictory customer confirmation).
8. References to sources, claims, journal and any meaningful budget limitations.

Closing a gap is **not** proving the missing answer; the associated claim cannot be upgraded without new evidence. A closed noncritical gap can be reopened if a material trigger occurs. This is a *gap-level* action only. Subphase and project closure require a separate [completion audit](completion-and-acceptance.md) and durable dossier.

## 4. Critical gaps and escalations

For a critical gap, Codex must not perform autonomous noncritical closure or invent historical certainty. Document the impacts, remaining evidence avenues and realistic alternatives, and escalate under the approved charter/plan rules. If the required answer cannot be supported, preserve a `blocked` or `escalated-critical` status and selectively pause only dependent work. Qualified completion or charter amendment requires the separate [completion rules](completion-and-acceptance.md) and applicable approval; a critical blocker cannot be reclassified to force success.

When charter assumptions materially fail, note affected question/plan dependencies and propose a human-approved amendment. Do not silently change the research objective.

## 5. Investigation journal — meaningful chronology

Record meaningful actions, findings, failures and decisions in a chronological journal. Every entry should distinguish **actual work** from planned/attempted activity. Include:

- stable journal entry anchor or consistent local sequence (journal entries are not automatically global concepts);
- ordering marker; timestamp only when actually known or provided (never fabricated);
- actor/action and contract question/lead;
- inspected source IDs and newly established claim/gap/concept references;
- failed search, access limitation or recovery attempt and its result;
- hypothesis/confidence or interpretation change;
- impact on dependencies, approvals and budget authorization;
- review triggers, next authorized steps and checkpoint impact.

Summarize repetitive low-value tool calls instead of transcribing logs. The journal is **not** a substitute for the canonical source register, claim evidence, decision ledger, or active checkpoint.

## 6. Record revisions and audit

Preserve important status changes with reasons and references. A new source may change a gap from `exhausted-noncritical` to `open`; a newly discovered contradiction may upgrade gap criticality. Do not overwrite the previous rationale without a trace.

Append journal entries in meaningful order and update source/claim/gap/concept records before indexes/checkpoint. Use scoped `GAP` allocation and never recycle IDs. The [templates](../templates/gap-register.md) and [journal](../templates/investigation-journal.md) provide examples; do not prepopulate them with invented company research.

## 7. Reporting and stop gate

At a research checkpoint report investigated contract questions, sources inspected, supporting/counterevidence, claim confidence, provisional interpretations, material gaps and their criticality, actual red-team review state and `RT` references (or **pending** where not performed), measured/estimated budget conditions and next authorized action. Do not state “completed” for a review not substantively performed or a subphase/project lacking its separate [completion audit](completion-and-acceptance.md) and dossier.
