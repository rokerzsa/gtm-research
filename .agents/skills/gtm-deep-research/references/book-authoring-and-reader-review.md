# Authoring and reader comprehension

Use when drafting or reviewing reader-facing prose. [Architecture](research-book-architecture.md) owns book structure; [synthesis](research-synthesis-and-lessons.md) owns analysis and the four review dimensions; [evidence](source-and-claim-evidence.md) owns factual support. These checks refine editorial review, not human approval gates.

## Establish what the reader needs next

Read the preceding ending alongside the chapter opening. Identify what the agreed reader already knows and what this chapter must establish. Introduce necessary identities, former names and relationships before relying on them; a short identification usually suffices. Do not assume that an overview's product list taught its workflow. Avoid repeating background the reader already has.

Give each chapter an explanatory responsibility: the question it answers and the new understanding it contributes. Keep details that develop that responsibility. Move a useful payment dispute, customer chronology or technical explanation to its primary treatment when it interrupts the current argument; retain a brief connection where needed. This is an editorial decision, not a new registry or mandatory planning artifact.

## Make the work understandable before judging the strategy

Explain enough of the actual interaction for the reader to describe who needs help, who initiates, what participants and the product do, what result is produced and how it is used. Include payment, human effort or coordination where they explain the choice. These are diagnostic questions, not required headings or a fictional customer scene.

Use the evidence already inspected, including useful workflow details buried in notes. If an essential step is unsupported, investigate within scope or state the limit. Never fill a gap with an invented workflow. Then explain how choices change customer value, delivery, selling and economics, distinguishing stated reasons from supported interpretation and results.

Order sections by understanding: establish the problem and work before interpreting constraints or comparing models. Chronology can show change, but do not invent causality or force overlapping offers into replacement stages. Tables consolidate grounded comparisons; they do not replace explanation. No fixed heading set, table quota or narrative ratio applies.

## Place uncertainty where it changes understanding

For each qualification ask: does this change what the reader should believe about this claim? Keep it nearby if it does. Attribution can establish that a statement is a founder's recollection without an additional audit disclaimer after every sentence. Detailed source comparisons, peripheral date disputes and access limits belong in notes unless central to the chapter's question. Consolidate repeated measurement gaps. Never remove a material dispute, imply certainty or upgrade confidence to improve flow.

## Review comprehension, not ingredient presence

Read main text sequentially without notes. Check whether the agreed reader can explain the actors and their relationships, actual workflow, customer before/after a change, reported difficulty, stated reasons versus interpretation, and what the chapter establishes beyond the overview. At each section boundary, ask why the next question follows. Close with the understanding gained and the question carried forward; the conclusion answers the introduction's inquiry.

When an answer depends on an unexplained name, missing step, notes or a later chapter, repair the dependency. Record concrete failures and repairs in the existing editorial review instead of merely checking that context, analysis and a transition are present. A same-author comprehension pass is not independent reader testing. When validating generic guidance, inspect both an origins passage and a later strategic change where applicable; disclose the scope actually tested.
