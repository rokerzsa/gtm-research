# Upfront discovery and execution agreement

[Book home](../book/index.md) · [Agreement template](../templates/research-charter.md) · [Authorization](activation-and-approval-gates.md)

## Reconnaissance before substantive research

Inspect enough background to ask informed questions: company/product names, current portfolio and buyer types, likely time boundaries, obvious strategic changes, important definitions and source limitations. Keep it bounded and provisional; distinguish accessed content from leads. This is not a preliminary full company history.

## Grill consequential ambiguity upfront

Use these thirteen dimensions as an internal completeness checklist, not thirteen required questions or approval rounds: motivation, objectives, existing knowledge, subject boundaries, depth, critical questions, assumptions, evidence standards, constraints, outputs, human involvement, completion criteria and exclusions.

Reuse context and group related questions. Ask targeted follow-ups until consequential choices are clear or the user explicitly accepts uncertainty. Challenge vague goals such as “what worked,” ambiguous first-customer definitions, unrealistic public-evidence expectations and assumed causal stories. Ask about preferences and decisions; do not demand historical facts the research should discover. If the user already supplied an adequate brief, do not repeat intake.

Confirm:
- Audience, purpose and desired analytical depth; general-reader language by default where appropriate.
- Subject/products/regions and cutoff; history plus current/emerging direction, or an explicitly historical-only study.
- Strategic questions and assumptions to challenge, not a predetermined success story.
- Full-company GTM overview plus adaptive deep dives; format and publication destination where relevant.
- Access/outreach constraints and real hard limits. Record absent numerical caps as unassigned, not a proposed arbitrary source quota.
- Which investigated unknowns may be delivered with qualifications and which user-defined must-have answers truly block delivery.
- Autonomous goal execution and internal reviews, stopping at final user review; optional later chapter-by-chapter review.

## One reviewable proposal

Present one agreement including the charter, full plan, internal-contract coverage, workspace root and explicit goal-start request. The user approves the package once; no separate charter/outline/phase approvals follow. Record exact decision evidence and versions before execution. An internal contract may be refined later under this approval if it stays within the objective and limits.

Do not invent preferences, goal authorization, numerical limits or user acceptance. Do not migrate an existing project's permissions automatically. Genuine changes to objective/subject or new external commitments need the relevant decision; ordinary strategic discoveries, outline changes and source tactics do not.
