# Chapter 01 — Research Modes and Charters

[Part I index](index.md) · [Book home](../index.md) · [Next: Chapter 02](02-governance-and-planning.md)

**Purpose:** Determine when substantial GTM research is necessary, clarify its objectives, and establish an explicitly approved and auditable charter before detailed planning.

**Governing decisions:** DR-001, DR-002, DR-007–DR-010, DR-046, DR-047.  
**Operational scope:** Research includes authorized investigation, independent red-team challenges, budgets, book synthesis/learning, and [criterion-based completion](../part-04-outputs-lifecycle/13-completion-and-acceptance.md). Live research is governed by its own charter, plan, workspace and budget approvals; documentation alone does not demonstrate runtime reliability.  
**Operational procedure:** [Goal discovery and charter](../../references/goal-discovery-and-charter.md).

## 1.1 Purpose and operating philosophy

GTM Deep Research is a **standalone Codex research capability** for investigating companies' market conditions, founding theses, early user and customer acquisition, distribution, pricing, sales, revenue, competition, unsuccessful experiments, growth mechanisms, and the evidence behind proposed causal explanations. Its methodology may generalize, but GTM is its primary domain.

The objective is to develop **defensible understanding**, not merely to produce persuasive reports. Codex must support both autonomous rigorous research and collaborative human learning. Their relative emphasis is configurable in the research charter; learning discussions and evidence determinations remain separate.

Codex must distinguish **finding information**, **verifying a record or report**, **establishing the underlying historical assertion**, **interpreting evidence**, and **deriving transferable lessons**. These are not interchangeable. An account that a founder made a statement is not proof of the historical content of that statement.

Research rigor is determined by the sufficiency, diversity, independence, and relevance of evidence and the examination of alternatives—not by source totals or the length of the final report.

## 1.2 Intent-based activation

Codex must assess the work before selecting the workflow. Evaluate:

1. **Complexity:** How many linked questions and dependencies must be resolved?
2. **Evidence uncertainty:** Is source discovery, provenance analysis or contradiction expected?
3. **Causal significance:** Does the question ask *why* or *how* outcomes occurred?
4. **Decision impact:** What could go wrong if an interpretation is unsupported?
5. **Expected effort:** Is a governed multi-phase investigation justified?

| Research mode | Appropriate behavior | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Lightweight | Direct explanation or targeted factual lookup without full charter | “When was HackerRank founded?” |
| Borderline | Apply the criteria and ask one targeted clarification only if needed | “Compare early pricing between two assessment platforms.” |
| Full GTM Deep Research | Begin bounded reconnaissance, mandatory discovery, charter approval and progressive planning | “Reconstruct the first 100 paying customers and test causal acquisition explanations.” |

A short answer may require deep research; a lengthy explanation may require little original investigation. **Requested word count must not determine research depth.** Activation does not grant permission to bypass approvals.

Substantive investigation may follow **only** after the charter, phase plan, detailed plan/contract and workspace gates are met; see [investigation execution](../../references/investigation-execution.md). Red-team follows [independent review](../../references/red-team-and-adjudication.md); project completion additionally follows [the charter-level audit](../../references/completion-and-acceptance.md).

## 1.3 Bounded preliminary reconnaissance

Before detailed goal-grilling, Codex may carry out **bounded reconnaissance** to understand terminology and context sufficiently to ask informed questions.

Reconnaissance should identify basic subject definitions, plausible market and product boundaries, obvious misconceptions, potentially relevant actors, likely evidence classes, and consequential unknowns.

For example, Codex may establish why “first users,” “first paying customers,” “first enterprise contracts” and “first recognized revenue” may refer to different events. It may identify archival pricing pages as a possible evidence avenue without assuming advertised prices equal contract revenue.

Reconnaissance must not become an unapproved attempt to complete the investigation. When information is encountered, preserve its provisional status and provenance, distinguishing inspected sources from third-party references or snippets. Keep the process proportionate to authorized time, spending and persistence availability.

Expected output: a **short discovery brief** with background, terms, assumptions, evidence unknowns and focused questions. Do not fabricate a historical narrative to make the brief appear complete.

## 1.4 Mandatory thirteen-dimension discovery framework

Every full research goal must address the following thirteen dimensions. Codex may group them into natural conversational rounds but cannot silently omit any dimension.

| # | Dimension | Required understanding |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Motivation | Why the user wants this research |
| 2 | Objectives | What understanding or decision the work should enable |
| 3 | Existing knowledge | What is already known, believed, assumed or disputed |
| 4 | Subject boundaries | Entities, time periods, markets, audiences, products and units of analysis |
| 5 | Depth | Required historical detail, causal rigor and granularity |
| 6 | Critical questions | Questions that must be answered or responsibly bounded |
| 7 | Assumptions | Hypotheses and beliefs that require challenge or verification |
| 8 | Evidence standards | Desired credibility, independence, access and uncertainty treatment |
| 9 | Constraints | Budget, time, ethical/access restrictions and resources |
| 10 | Outputs | OKF knowledge, readable book, analysis and possible learning artifacts |
| 11 | Human involvement | Approval expectations, learning intensity and collaboration |
| 12 | Completion criteria | Objective conditions for legitimate closure, including allowed limitations |
| 13 | Exclusions | Explicit non-goals and out-of-scope work |

If a dimension is inapplicable, Codex should mark it as such with a short explanation. It should not invent user preferences or research budgets to fill gaps.

The generic core is mandatory. Codex may propose GTM-specific extensions, such as first 1/10/100 customers, distribution advantage, retention, monetization, failed channel experiments or temporal source triangulation, when justified. **Consequential extensions require human approval** and cannot silently broaden an existing charter.

## 1.5 Rigorous adaptive goal-grilling

Discovery is an iterative reasoning dialogue, not a single questionnaire. Codex must actively challenge consequential ambiguities, weak premises, misleading success narratives, missing alternatives, hidden assumptions, unrealistic evidence expectations and internal conflicts.

It should ask *why the distinction matters* and present plausible options. Examples:

- Does “first customer” mean the first registered user, paying customer, signed enterprise contract or first recognized revenue?
- Is the user seeking a factual chronology, a causal model of growth, or a playbook for another company?
- Could observed acquisition have resulted from founder access, product-led adoption, a market-wide surge, partners, or simultaneous mechanisms?
- What happens if direct records of the first 100 customers cannot be obtained, but independent partial evidence is available?

Question rounds continue until consequential ambiguities are resolved or explicitly accepted as uncertainty. Codex must not force agreement on a convenient hypothesis or claim that a chosen research framing proves the intended history.

## 1.6 Versioned research charter

Every full Deep Research project must have a **versioned and auditable charter**. The charter governs the investigation's objectives and authorization, not its actual historical conclusions.

| Required section | Content |
|---|---|
| Identity and motivation | Research title, subject, context and reason for inquiry |
| Objectives and scope | Desired understanding, included topics, boundaries and exclusions |
| Questions and priorities | Mandatory questions and criticality |
| Assumptions and known uncertainty | Provisional hypotheses, unknowns and dependencies |
| Evidence standards | Source independence, verification requirements and permitted limitations |
| Constraints | Access, time, effort, spending and discretion bounds |
| Human participation | Approval gates, review and learning preferences |
| Deliverables | Authoritative OKF evidence, concepts, reader-facing book and appendices |
| Completion criteria | Objective completion conditions and allowed qualified outcomes |
| Governance | Explicit approval, amendment history, rationale and related decisions |

The charter must distinguish **approved requirements** from open questions, provisional hypotheses and future proposals. Human approval of a research goal cannot be inferred from a plausible plan.

Changes that materially affect approved scope, objectives, critical questions or completion conditions require explicit approval and versioned amendment history. A heavyweight requirements traceability framework is unnecessary; concise references between charter, contracts, decisions and evidence are enough.

The charter and its approval records are owned by the standalone GTM OKF workspace, not by Goal Memory or any separate goal-management system.

Use the [charter authoring template](../../templates/research-charter.md) and [authorization procedure](../../references/activation-and-approval-gates.md).

## 1.7 Goal approval is separate from plan approval

Codex must present a sufficiently complete charter and obtain an explicit decision identifying the approved version. If the workspace has been authorized and initialized, save the decision with actual human approval evidence. If safe durable recording is unavailable, do not pretend an approval record exists; repair or initialize before dependent stateful work.

**Goal/charter approval does not approve a high-level phase plan or an immediately executable detailed plan.** The following chapter defines those separate gates.

Chapter 01 is satisfied when the human and researcher share an authorized definition of what will be investigated, why it matters, the boundaries and evidence rules, and the objective criteria for completion—not when all the research questions have already been answered.
