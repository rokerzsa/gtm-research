# Chapter 09 — Shared Knowledge and Dependencies

[Previous: Chapter 08](08-research-records-and-identity.md) · [Part III](index.md) · [Book home](../index.md) · [Next: Chapter 10](../part-04-outputs-lifecycle/10-research-synthesis-and-lessons.md)

**Governing decisions:** DR-027, DR-038, DR-039, DR-045. **Purpose:** Reuse evidence responsibly across GTM research projects, identify consequential upstream changes, selectively reassess affected conclusions and reopen completed work when warranted, without assuming continuous background monitoring.

## 9.1 Shared authoritative knowledge

The standalone research workspace makes inspected source provenance (`SRC`), claim/evidence records (`CLM`) and semantic concepts (`K`) reusable across studies. Projects should reference stable existing records instead of copying the same material into independent, conflicting sources of truth. Retain original provenance, time/subject scope, source lineage, contradictions, confidence and procedural review history. Reuse is not an automatic endorsement or a second independent corroboration.

## 9.2 Contextual revalidation

Before relying materially on existing knowledge in a new project, verify its relevance to the receiving question, time period, claim definition and permitted evidence standard. Reassess its current confidence, conflicting evidence and source independence. A previously supported old pricing page may establish an advertised offer, not customer-realized revenue; an earlier causal explanation does not automatically qualify as reviewed in a broader comparative strategy study. Major receiving-project conclusions must receive the receiving project's mandatory [independent red-team review](../part-02-investigation/05-independent-red-team-review.md).

## 9.3 Lightweight consequential dependency register

Record **important analytical reliance**, not every hyperlink. A dependency links stable upstream `K` or qualified `PRJ:SRC/CLM` and downstream claims, concepts, contracts, book chapters or projects, with its type, significance, last validated status and revisit trigger. Maintain an upstream-to-known-dependents lookup in a [dependency impact register](../../templates/dependency-impact-register.md). Avoid a full automated knowledge graph or needless duplication of evidence. State explicitly when dependency discovery is incomplete.

## 9.4 Material-change threshold

A material upstream change may include new contradictory contemporary evidence, correction to an early-customer definition or key date, disclosure that multiple accounts all derive from one interview, weakened confidence, red-team disconfirmation, invalidated causal assumptions or a newly undermined completion basis. Editorial wording, formatting and path-only movement with preserved identities do not ordinarily trigger reopening.

Assess impact on the approved research objective, causal/strategic conclusions, downstream dependencies, review requirements and approved completion criteria. The size of the edit is not a reliable proxy for materiality.

## 9.5 Selective invalidation

On a credible material change: record the new source and claim, locate known dependents via stable references, document materiality and affected scope, flag dependents `reassessment pending`, selectively pause only work dependent on invalidated assumptions, and continue unrelated authorized investigation. Then determine required new evidence, review, plan and budget authorization; adjudicate and revise affected claims/concepts/book sections. Invalidation does not automatically mark a dependent historical statement false.

Changes affecting a major causal conclusion may require a fresh genuinely independent `RT` pass. Before clearing an impact flag, verify that current evidence and review states actually justify the new claim, then repair indexes/revision history and update the active checkpoint last. See [impact and reopening procedure](../../references/dependency-impact-and-reopening.md).

## 9.6 Reopening completed subphases

Codex may reopen an affected completed subphase if newly encountered credible evidence materially changes a major conclusion, important dependency or documented contract completion basis. Preserve its prior versioned completion dossier and closure reasoning. Create a specific [reopening record](../../templates/reopening-record.md) with trigger, impact and revised required work. Minor evidence updates may be integrated into existing records without reopening the entire subphase. Repeat affected review and completion checks; do not restart unrelated subphases.

## 9.7 Reopening completed projects

When credible evidence encountered during authorized work materially undermines a completed project's major conclusion, critical dependency or charter-level completion basis, Codex may autonomously record its project state as `reopened — assessment pending`, after persisting the trigger and old completion dossier. This changes **lifecycle state**, not the original historical fact or scope authorization. All work requiring additional detailed planning, charter change, resources or paid access still needs the existing human approvals. Preserve previous project completion as a past version rather than erasing it. Reclosure requires current valid criteria, mandatory reviews and a new completion dossier under [Chapter 13](../part-04-outputs-lifecycle/13-completion-and-acceptance.md).

## 9.8 Cross-project example (illustrative only)

Suppose Study A reported that a software assessment company began enterprise sales in year X. Study B reused that conclusion in a commercialization timeline. A later inspected contemporary record shows year X referred only to an advertised product rather than a paid contract. Codex should correct the underlying claim, record the source and old interpretation, locate B's material reliance, mark B's comparative timeline for reassessment, and update only conclusions whose support changes. Neither study automatically loses all its findings; B's new causal comparison, if major, gets its own applicable red-team scrutiny.

## 9.9 Monitoring and authorization boundary

Autonomous reopening is triggered **when the skill encounters new evidence** or under a separately authorized monitoring process. This chapter does not make Codex a continuously running agent. Neither reopening nor selective pausing authorizes unapproved spending, research scope, a new contract or plan. If material dependence cannot be searched confidently, the impact report must disclose that coverage limitation.

## 9.10 Recoverable revision order

Persist upstream evidence and review changes → impact/reopening records and retained prior dossiers → affected claim/concept/book corrections, reference indexes and journal → consistency checks → project checkpoint last. If interrupted, preserve the pending revalidation state rather than silently restoring `complete`. [Recovery procedure](../../references/recovery-and-consistency.md).

## 9.11 Completion of dependency/lifecycle requirements

Reusing knowledge must maintain provenance and contextual scrutiny. Material changes must identify known affected dependents, avoid false certainty, support selective repairs/reopening and retain original completion history. New scope/spending and background monitoring never follow automatically from a changed record.
