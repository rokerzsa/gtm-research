# Chapter 13 — Completion and Acceptance

[Previous: Chapter 12](12-human-learning.md) · [Part IV](index.md) · [Book home](../index.md) · [Next: Chapter 14](../part-05-implementation/14-skill-implementation-and-review.md)

**Governing decisions:** DR-026, DR-037. **Purpose:** Define objective and auditable autonomous completion of authorized research subphases and projects without pretending that all historical questions have certain answers.

## 13.1 Completion is a condition, not a narrative impression

Completion means the *current explicitly approved* research contract or charter completion conditions were satisfied or legitimately disposed of as the charter permits. It is not the same as consuming a budget, producing a long report, accumulating citations, receiving a red-team `passed` label or obtaining a person's agreement. Unverified historical claims stay unverified after administrative completion. The skill may complete work autonomously when objective gates pass; it does not need an extra final human acceptance by default.

## 13.2 Subphase completion audit

For each approved `RC` and its exact charter/high-level/detailed plan versions, Codex must verify: mandatory question outcomes; appropriate source provenance and accessible supporting and counterevidence; evidence state and qualitative confidence; completed required major/aggregate `RT` challenges and bounded adjudications; allowed gap disposition; affected dependencies and required output concepts; budget authorization and measured/estimated limitations; durable journal/index/book contributions; and absence of unresolved recovery or required human decisions. A completed subphase must be explainable through a versioned [completion dossier](../../templates/subphase-completion-dossier.md).

Noncritical evidence-exhausted gaps may receive documented autonomous closure; a **critical gap** or consequential red-team dispute that violates current completion criteria cannot be renamed into a qualified success. If the charter permits a qualified outcome after the mandated escalation/disposition, preserve the explicit caveat. If not, mark the work blocked/incomplete pending a real authorized decision or amendment. Human approval can amend a future completion criterion but cannot establish past events as certain.

## 13.3 Subphase dossier and persistence

The dossier records the approved contract/version, questions and evidence chain, review outcomes, criticality of gaps, explicit limitations, dependencies, required OKF outputs, budget usage and a criterion-by-criterion reasoned result. Use `complete`, `complete with qualifications` only when authorized, or `blocked/incomplete`. Preserve previous versions when later reopened. Persist the dossier, dependent indexes and journal; validate; then advance the authoritative active-state checkpoint **last**. A draft dossier is not an actual closure.

## 13.4 Phase progression is separately authorized

Autonomous completion of a phase or subphase does not approve a new immediate detailed plan. A subsequent dependent subphase requiring detailed plan/contract approval must wait for that human gate. Independent already-approved work may continue. Do not use an accomplished milestone to infer new budget authorization.

## 13.5 Final project charter audit

At the project level, Codex must evaluate the approved objective against **all** charter mandatory questions and completion criteria, not merely verify that the original task list was exhausted. Check all required subphase dossiers, important evidence/counterevidence/confidence, all mandatory red-team/adjudication dispositions, material unresolved critical blockers, invalidated assumptions, known shared dependencies, authorized scope changes, and resource/budget status. Validate that the Docusaurus-style Markdown-first OKF book has the agreed high-level structure, correct indexes and chapter links, current evidence-linked conclusions, limitations and revision history. Missing decisive evidence must remain visible.

## 13.6 Autonomous project completion

If the audit passes, Codex may mark the project `complete` or `complete with qualifications` (only if current charter terms permit that disposition) and present its [project completion dossier](../../templates/project-completion-dossier.md) for review. The dossier includes the approved charter/version, research questions/outcomes, completed phases and contracts, significant reviewed conclusions, unresolved limitations, major budget decisions and actual/estimated/unavailable spending, deliverable book links, dependencies, revision history and reason for closure. No separate final human sign-off is required unless the charter expressly mandates one. This does not waive human approval for changed charter, plan or resource authorization.

## 13.7 Blocked and incomplete outcomes

If a critical gap, pending mandatory review, material unresolved disagreement, missing actual approval, invalid book evidence link or inconsistent recovery state prevents a mandatory criterion, leave it `blocked/incomplete`. Document precise blocker, attempted avenues, affected conclusions and next human decision if needed. Resource ceilings trigger reassessment or permission requests, **not** completion. Avoid inventing results or suppressing unresolved contradictions merely to achieve a final state.

## 13.8 Subsequent material evidence

A complete project is not continuously monitored by default. When credible new evidence is later encountered, apply [Chapter 09](../part-03-okf-architecture/09-shared-knowledge-and-dependencies.md): minor changes update existing evidence/book records; material changes can reopen affected scope with old dossiers preserved. Reopening does not itself authorize new money, scope or detailed plans. A reclosed project receives a new dossier and version while the prior completion remains auditable.

## 13.9 Presentation and epistemic boundary

Report to the human which charter criteria passed, key conclusions and their confidence, competing interpretations, reviewed major claims, remaining gaps, budget measurement status and readable book/dossier access. **Research completion** and **skill release/installation** are different decisions: the latter remains an explicit human gate under [Chapter 14](../part-05-implementation/14-skill-implementation-and-review.md).
