## 1. Motivation

Understand RedyHire's historical go-to-market development as a company case study. The user explicitly selected historical GTM. No current growth consulting, investment decision, or application to another business is assumed.

## 2. Objectives

Reconstruct what RedyHire sold, who used and bought it, how early customers were acquired, how sales and monetization developed, which experiments worked or failed, and why material changes occurred. Explain causal mechanisms where evidence supports them and preserve competing explanations where it does not.

Derive specific historical lessons and their limits from RedyHire's record. Describe the business and customer workflow before drawing lessons.

## 4. Subject boundaries

- **Entity:** RedyHire at redyhire.com and associated legal entities, founders, products, customers, and relevant partners. Establish continuity with RedyHire Technologies LLP rather than assuming it. Exclude similarly named RedHire companies.
- **Period:** earliest relevant origin activity through 11 November 2026. Investigate the reported 2019 formation and venture story around 2024 separately. Earlier context is included only where needed to explain origins or distribution advantages.
- **Geography:** India and evidenced overseas activity; distinguish international plans, partnerships, and actual customer deployments.
- **Products:** screening, assessments, interviews, hiring workflow, and recruiting services where historically demonstrated. Distinguish a live capability from an announcement or marketing claim.
- **Units:** company and product milestones, customer accounts, acquisition channels, and buying workflows. Separate first registration, active usage, paying customer, signed contract, and revenue received.

## 5. Depth

A substantial evidence-linked historical case study, comparable in reader usefulness to the existing HackerRank book. Seek account-level early-customer detail where supportable; do not promise an arbitrary first-100 customer reconstruction. Cover chronology and causal mechanisms without equating business success with a particular channel or product feature.

## 6. Critical questions

1. What existed before the present venture story, and how do legal formation, founder involvement, product development, and commercial launch connect?
2. What problem did the first product solve, for whom, and against which alternatives?
3. Who were the earliest identifiable users and paying customers? What is substantiated about their discovery, evaluation, purchase, deployment, and payment?
4. Who were the user, champion, economic buyer, and budget owner? How did the product fit their hiring workflow?
5. Which channels demonstrably generated customers, and how did founder relationships, referrals, outbound, content, partners, and other experiments contribute?
6. How did pricing, packaging, trials, onboarding, services, sales cycles, retention, and account expansion change?
7. What drove changes in product scope, positioning, target segments, and geography? Which changes preceded outcomes and which remain ambitions?
8. What failed, stalled, or constrained growth, and what actions followed?
9. What can be established about customer scale, revenue, funding, retention, hiring outcomes, and economics?
10. Which explanations survive independent adversarial source investigation, alternative causes, and explicit falsification conditions?

Origins, first commercial traction, acquisition mechanisms, monetization, and material changes are the core priorities. If first-payment identity, channel attribution, or commercial metrics remain inaccessible, document the failed avenues and evidence boundary instead of inventing precision.

## 7. Assumptions to test

Referral acquisition and strong support are founder-reported hypotheses; test their time boundary, completeness, and relationship to actual sales and retention. Assessment-to-orchestration may reflect product expansion, repositioning, or an unproven ambition. Tenant career pages can show usage without proving payment or results. Bootstrap, break-even, accuracy, fairness, and efficiency assertions require definitions and evidence appropriate to each claim.

Consider alternatives including market timing, founder reputation, service effort, existing customer relationships, product breadth, pricing, and competitor changes. Do not impose a success narrative.

## 8. Evidence standards

Prioritize contemporary company artifacts, customer-originated evidence, official records, dated product/pricing pages, credible independent reporting, and relevant primary competitor documentation. Attribute retrospectives and compare them with contemporaneous material. Separate discovered sources, inspected content, snippets, secondhand descriptions, and failed access.

Maintain source provenance, claim evidence and counterevidence, qualitative confidence, material gaps, and separate review state. Several retellings of one original account are one lineage. Major individual and aggregate conclusions require independent source-seeking adversarial review with alternatives and falsification conditions. Comparative feature claims require matched dates and specific capabilities.

## 12. Completion criteria

- Every critical question has a supported answer or an expressly reviewed evidence limitation permitted by this charter.
- The story explains the product, hiring workflow, users, buyers, acquisition, economics, outcomes, and reasons for major changes.
- Material conclusions trace to source/claim records and receive required independent adversarial review.
- Incorporation versus launch, use versus payment, advertised versus realized pricing, and reported versus independently corroborated outcomes remain distinct.
- Consequential disputes and critical gaps are addressed explicitly; no unsupported central historical conclusion is presented as established.
- The connected book, record integrity, navigation, budget, dependencies, and closure criteria pass documented audits.

Approval of this charter permits a qualified final history where reasonable public-source avenues are exhausted and otherwise inaccessible facts remain clearly unknown. It does not permit treating budget exhaustion as evidence exhaustion or silently dropping central questions.

## 13. Exclusions

Current growth recommendations, investment valuation, legal advice, exhaustive HR-tech market sizing, live product/security benchmarking, person-level prospect databases, application to another business, external interviews/outreach, paid data, and automatic publication/deployment are outside scope. Competitor and market research is included only to explain RedyHire's historical choices and test material comparative claims.
