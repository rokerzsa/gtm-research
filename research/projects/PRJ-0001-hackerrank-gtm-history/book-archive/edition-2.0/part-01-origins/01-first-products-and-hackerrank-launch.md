# Changing the problem, buyer and delivery model

The important early change was from helping a candidate prepare to helping an employer evaluate applicants. **That changed the buyer's reason to pay and the work InterviewStreet had to deliver.** It is more informative than treating the company's development as a sequence of new names.

[Complete company GTM system](../part-00-gtm-system/00-full-gtm-system.md)

## The operating constraint behind the first model

Mock interviews combined candidate demand with an experienced person's time. In Vivek's retrospective account, payment collection, scheduling, interviewer incentives and the margin after compensation made the service difficult to operate. Those are stated constraints, not an independently audited account of the business. [1](#note-1)

The strategic problem was therefore not simply whether someone wanted interview help. The company also needed a repeatable way to supply it economically. Improving scheduling or interviewer supply would have addressed the existing model; moving to employer tests changed both the task and the customer.

## Compare the choices, not just their dates

| Dimension | Candidate practice | Employer assessment | Strategic implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| Immediate problem | Prepare one person for an interview | Decide which applicants deserve interview time | Employer value can be explained through existing hiring work. |
| Delivery | Arrange a person to conduct practice | Send a programming exercise and evaluate code | Software can make part of the work repeatable without a practice interviewer per session. |
| Commercial unit | A candidate's practice session | Applicant-testing activity | Charging can follow a different customer's workload. |
| New difficulty | Attract and serve candidates | Reach a hiring team and fit its process | Product change does not remove the need for distribution. |

Archived Recruit pages show the employer offer by December 2010. They establish an available offer and advertised pricing, not every sale or an exact day when all earlier experiments ended. [2](#note-2)

The operational comparison suggests a possible reduction in coordination work. It does not prove a founder motive, realized margin improvement or universal superiority of employer software. The stronger founder-stated value proposition concerned screening and time saved in hiring. [1](#note-1)

## Employer access was another problem to solve

Early accounts describe introductions, referrals, demonstrations and recruiter inbound. WisdomTap's reported referral relationship and Flipkart's use make the route into hiring work concrete; BankBazaar is the founder-reported first cheque. Their differing milestones should not be collapsed into one verified first-paying-customer event. [3](#note-3)

These accounts support a distribution interpretation: relationships helped put an unfamiliar product in front of people who could evaluate it. They do not establish how much eventual revenue each route generated. The [commercial-model chapter](../part-02-first-customers/02-first-customers-and-monetization.md) examines what the relationships and prices actually tell us.

## A second change: finding candidates as well as testing them

A private assessment starts with an applicant the employer already knows about. A public contest can introduce programmers the employer has not reached. CodeSprint accounts show distinct candidate steps—participation, application and interview progression—while founder accounts describe sourcing as an important opportunity. [4](#note-4) [5](#note-5)

That expanded the product's possible role. It also introduced a different strategic task: give programmers a reason to participate outside an immediate job application. Practice and competition could do that. Building participation and selling an employer relationship remained different jobs.

The evidence supports overlapping screening, sourcing and community offers rather than one clean replacement pivot. Brand consolidation connected them, but a shared brand did not make users, candidates and paying employers interchangeable. [6](#note-6) [7](#note-7)

## What this change establishes

The early business changed its customer problem, delivery model and charging opportunity, then widened toward candidate discovery. Those are meaningful strategic changes. The exact product overlap and first payer remain uncertain, and the community's contribution to paid employer acquisition is not established. [8](#note-8)

The transferable insight is to examine the coupled choices: a new buyer can bring a clearer reason to spend, while requiring new selling capabilities; a more repeatable product can change delivery without proving demand. Both sides of that decision belong in the analysis.
