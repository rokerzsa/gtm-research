## 1. Motivation and context

Build a historical GTM case study that explains HackerRank’s development from InterviewStreet through its developer community and employer products, and derives transferable lessons with clear limits and conditions.

This is for historical learning, not to decide a specific current business action.

## 2. Research objectives

**Primary objective:** Reconstruct the company’s historical GTM and identify evidence-supported causal mechanisms behind its product and commercial evolution.

**Secondary objectives:** Understand early customer acquisition, community distribution, B2B monetization, sales and pricing, later shifts, and failures.

**Decisions this may inform:** Transferable GTM learning; no specific current-company decision is in scope.

## 3. Existing knowledge and misconceptions

The user starts without a prior view. The provisional leads and their source status are recorded in the pre-charter discovery brief. The reported early Flipkart customer milestone and the hypothesis that developer-community growth caused employer sales are explicit claims to test, not accepted findings.

The initial reconnaissance identified differing descriptions of early product sequence and customer milestones. Those accounts require corroboration and careful definitions.

| Boundary | Included | Excluded or uncertain |

## 5. Required depth

Use a causal historical reconstruction. Investigate early customers and paying accounts, pricing and revenue models, acquisition channels, product changes, failures, and later strategic shifts. Pursue 10- or 100-customer milestones only when evidence supports them; no exhaustive customer roster is required.

## 7. Assumptions and alternatives

| Assumption / hypothesis | Provisional status | Competing explanation or falsification avenue |
|---|---|---|
| Developer community growth supported employer acquisition. | Unverified | Community may have been a parallel product or engagement channel; test direct sales, founder networks, events, and referrals. |
| The company moved from candidate products to employer software in one discrete pivot. | Unverified | Products may have overlapped or evolved incrementally. |
| Reported early customer milestones use consistent definitions. | Unverified | Distinguish trials, paying accounts, contracts, revenue, and customer logos. |

## 8. Evidence standards

Prefer contemporaneous primary sources. Independently corroborate material historical claims where possible, label retrospective and reported claims accurately, retain contradictions, and use no fixed source-count quota. Assess source independence and information lineage. A reported statement does not by itself verify the underlying event.

**Major conclusions:** Causal and strategic conclusions require an independent red-team source investigation. Review outcomes do not automatically change claim confidence or evidence state.

**Acceptable evidence-exhausted limitations:** Clearly qualified limitations may be reported when original evidence cannot be recovered. Unresolved critical gaps block project completion.

**Unacceptable shortcuts:** No fabricated access, no customer or revenue inference from logos or anecdotes, and no causal conclusion from correlation or repeated retellings of the same source.

## 9. Constraints and budgets

**Access:** No paid access. Use freely accessible public sources. External interviews or outreach require separate approval.

**Effort and source limits:** No numerical cap has been assigned. A measurable proposal may be presented in a later plan for explicit review; this charter grants no preauthorized extension.

**Dependencies:** Charter approval is recorded. High-level plan approval, immediate detailed plan approval, and an attached research contract remain separate gates.

## 10. Deliverables

**Authoritative knowledge:** Source provenance, claim-level evidence, gaps, semantic concepts, decisions, and auditable project records in the standalone OKF workspace.

**Reader-facing synthesis:** A Markdown-first, Docusaurus-style research book covering the company’s origins and product evolution, early customers, community distribution, monetization and sales, later shifts, causal adjudication, and transferable lessons.

**Indexes:** Evidence-linked navigation and source/claim references. Docusaurus includes only files under projects/**/book/**/*.md; workspace controls and evidence records are not listed as reader-facing docs.

**Learning outputs:** Deep, nonblocking learning checkpoints at phase boundaries.

## 13. Explicit exclusions and non-goals

No valuation or investor-return model, exhaustive competitor histories, or external interviews/outreach unless separately approved. Competitor evidence is included only where it helps explain HackerRank’s GTM. Candidate interview-preparation advice is out of scope.

## 14. Human-approved subject-specific extension

Early customer and paying-account milestones are in scope. Later 10/100-customer milestones are included only where evidence supports them, as approved during discovery. No unapproved scope additions are authorized.

## 15. Open questions and accepted uncertainty

Reconcile differing accounts of the early timeline, product sequence, and first-customer claims. Separate user, customer, contract, and revenue definitions. Current 2026 product and pricing changes remain to be verified through authorized research.
