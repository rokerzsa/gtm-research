## A. High-level research phase outline

**Status:** Approved v1 by PRJ-0001:DEC-0002.

**Objective and charter alignment:** Reconstruct HackerRank’s GTM from early InterviewStreet origins through 2026, establish early customer and monetization milestones, assess acquisition mechanisms and alternatives, and derive evidence-linked lessons.

| Phase | Objective and mandatory question groups | Dependencies | Outputs / reviews | Indicative allocation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| P1 — Origins and product iterations | Establish the origin context, early customer problem, product versions, and sequence of changes; define key terms and dates. | None | Source/claim records and a provisional origin timeline; flag high-impact chronology claims for review. | P1-v1 / RC-0001: maximum 20 inspected source investigations; no paid access. |
| P2 — First customers and early monetization | Investigate first users versus first paying organizations, the Flipkart report, early account acquisition, price/contract distinctions, and first commercial model. | P1 | Early-customer and monetization evidence map; preserve unsupported milestones as gaps. | Not assigned; no paid access. |
| P3 — Community and employer GTM | Investigate HackerRank’s public community, contests, sponsored challenges, employer products, referrals, founder-led selling, and sales organization; assess later 10/100-customer claims only where supportable. | P1 and P2 | Channel and customer-conversion analysis; independent review of material community-to-employer causal conclusions. | Not assigned; no paid access. |
| P4 — Scale and later GTM shifts | Trace material global expansion, upmarket motion, product broadening, pricing and strategic shifts from 2018 through 2026. | P3 | Later evolution timeline and comparison to earlier GTM; assess current product changes only where material. | Not assigned; no paid access. |
| P5 — Causal adjudication and synthesis | Compare mechanisms across phases, examine failures and rival explanations, and derive conditional transferable lessons. | P1–P4 | Independent red-team reviews of major conclusions, evidence-linked book, indexes, and completion dossier. | Not assigned; no paid access. |

**Phase ordering rationale:** Establish product and customer chronology before explaining growth. Separate early customer acquisition from later community and enterprise scale. Synthesize only after the evidence and alternative explanations across phases are reviewed.

**Known risks and uncertainties:** Retrospective founder narratives may compress overlapping products; customer counts and “first customer” claims may use different definitions; repeated press coverage may share one source lineage; no paid sources or external outreach are authorized. The P1 ceiling is 20 inspected source investigations; later-phase numeric limits remain unassigned.

**Permissible progressive elaboration:** Detailed phase plans and contracts will be prepared only after this high-level outline is approved. Phase boundaries remain binding; later phases may be refined when current findings justify it.

### B.1 Objective and boundaries

Reconstruct the company’s origin context and early product sequence from reported origins around 2009 through the 2012 HackerRank launch. Distinguish what contemporaneous materials show from later founder recollections.

This phase identifies early customer and monetization claims for later work but does not attempt a complete first-customer audit, a first-100-customer reconstruction, or a broad competitor history. Those are reserved for P2 or excluded by the charter.

### B.2 Mandatory questions

| Question | Expected answer/evidence condition | Criticality |
|---|---|---|
| What problem and user/customer were the founders initially addressing? | Contemporaneous evidence where available, with retrospective claims attributed separately. | Critical |
| What were the early product iterations and when were they visible? | A dated sequence distinguishing product launch, company/brand name, and public site availability. | Critical |
| How did the initial candidate and employer workflows coexist or change? | Specific product evidence; do not assume a single pivot. | Critical |
| What early distribution and acquisition approaches are evidenced? | Distinguish coding challenges, events, YC/founder networks, referrals, and recruiter workflows; record what each source actually supports. | Priority |
| What early customer, pricing, and revenue claims should feed P2? | Record as reported leads or provisional claims; verify payment, contract, and revenue separately. | Priority |

### B.3 Known dependencies and assumptions

- Approved charter v1 and high-level plan v1 are in force.
- The pre-charter discovery brief contains leads, not project evidence records.
- Product changes may have overlapped; founder retrospectives may compress chronology.
- The reported early customer and pricing statements must not be treated as verified contract or revenue facts.

### B.4 Investigation strategy (planned, not executed)

Prioritize contemporaneous primary materials: company launch pages, archived product pages, dated company announcements, public accelerator materials, and first-party customer or partner statements where freely accessible. Use contemporaneous independent reporting to triangulate. Use founder interviews and profiles as attributed retrospective accounts. Inspect original sources and preserve source lineage; search-result snippets alone do not count as inspected evidence.

**Competing hypotheses:** A direct evolution from candidate preparation to employer assessment; overlapping candidate and recruiter products; community/event-led acquisition; founder-network or direct-sales-led acquisition.

**Evidence integrity constraints:** No paid sources, no external outreach, and no inference from customer logos or user counts to contracts or revenue.

### B.5 Review triggers and learning gates

Any major causal or strategic conclusion receives an independent source-seeking red-team review under the charter. Keep early chronology claims provisional until contradictions and source lineage are assessed. Offer a deep, nonblocking learning checkpoint at the P1 boundary.

### B.6 Budget and authorized exploration

**Approved P1 ceiling:** At most 20 distinct source investigations actually inspected, authorized by PRJ-0001:DEC-0003. This is an upper limit, not a source quota or target; stop earlier when the approved questions are answered or no useful avenue remains. Count the source material actually inspected, not search-result URLs; mirrors of the same underlying material share one investigation. No preauthorized extension.

Pre-charter reconnaissance is outside the P1 cap; any reconnaissance source re-inspected or formally integrated as P1 evidence counts once. Paid access remains unauthorized. No elapsed-time or monetary research budget has been assigned.

### B.7 Required OKF outputs and completion tests

Create only records that are materially needed: source register, claim register, gap register, and investigation journal. Create a semantic concept if a reusable, substantive synthesis warrants one. Draft an evidence-linked book section on origins and early product iterations only from persisted source and claim records.

P1 may close only after the chronology and key product distinctions are addressed or qualified, material source/claim links validate, failed recovery avenues and unresolved gaps are recorded, budget usage is reconciled, and any required independent review is complete or explicitly remains a blocker. P1 completion does not complete the overall project.

### B.8 Dependency and change controls

Stop at the approved source ceiling. Do not use paid access or conduct outreach. If a critical question requires scope expansion, a budget extension, or a new access method, record the proposal and seek separate approval before acting. Material contradiction of a charter assumption pauses only dependent work and requires the charter/plan amendment process.
