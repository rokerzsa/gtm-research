## 1. Motivation

The user requested research on RedyHire using the GTM deep research skill. The immediate reason and intended decision are awaiting clarification. Proposed default: understand how the company acquired customers and how its commercial motion changed. Historical case study, present growth recommendations, and business/product diligence are materially different objectives; select the intended one before finalizing v1.

## 2. Objectives

Proposed primary objective: reconstruct RedyHire's GTM development from the earliest relevant activity through 11 November 2026, explaining the product, customer problem, first users and paying customers, acquisition, sales, monetization, retention, experiments, and constraints.

Proposed secondary objective: derive conditional lessons from RedyHire's evidence, with alternative causal explanations and explicit unknowns. Application to another business is outside this proposed scope. Recommendations for RedyHire itself require selecting that objective and defining the decision context.

## 4. Subject boundaries

Proposed subject: RedyHire at redyhire.com and the associated RedyHire Technologies LLP, with identity continuity investigated rather than assumed. Include relevant predecessor activity from 2019 when it explains the current business; distinguish legal formation, product launch, first user, first paying account, contract signing, and revenue collection.

Geography: India and any overseas sales/partnership activity supported by evidence. Product boundaries: employer screening, skills assessment, interviews, hiring workflow, and any demonstrable recruiting services. Exclude similarly named RedHire businesses.

## 5. Depth

Proposed depth: a substantial historical case study with dated milestones, account-level early customer reconstruction where supportable, commercial workflow explanation, and causal analysis that tests referral, founder relationships, product quality, service effort, pricing, market timing, and channel execution as competing explanations.

## 6. Mandatory questions

1. What existed before the current startup story, and how do entity, founder, product, and launch dates connect?
2. What did the earliest product do, for whom, and against which alternatives?
3. Who were the earliest users and actual paying customers? What are the first substantiated sale, contract, and payment events?
4. How did customers discover, evaluate, buy, onboard, use, renew, and expand the product? Who was user, champion, buyer, and budget owner?
5. Which channels demonstrably produced customers, and which were experiments or promotional activity without proven conversion?
6. How did pricing, packaging, trials, services, sales process, and geographic reach change?
7. What explains the movement between assessment, interviewing, and orchestration, and which capabilities were available versus announced?
8. Which experiments failed or encountered limits? What changed as a result?
9. What scale, revenue, retention, hiring outcomes, and unit economics can be established or responsibly bounded?
10. Which conclusions survive independent source investigation, competing explanations, and explicit falsification tests?

First-customer identities, payment order, channel attribution, and causal commercial results are priority questions. If public evidence cannot establish them, the proposed output must explain the search limits and retain the unknowns; acceptance of that completion condition is pending.

## 7. Assumptions and alternatives

- Referral-led acquisition is a reported narrative; test its completeness, time boundary, and distinction from founder-assisted selling or services.
- A broader platform message may describe product expansion, repackaging, or an ambition; do not infer a successful pivot from current copy.
- Named customer pages can show usage without proving payment, renewal, outcomes, or customer count.
- Bootstrap/break-even and efficiency/accuracy assertions require definitions and provenance; vendor reporting alone does not independently establish them.

## 8. Evidence standards

Prioritize contemporary first-party artifacts, customer-originated records, official filings, dated product/pricing material, and credible independent reporting. Use retrospectives with attribution and compare them against contemporary records. Record discovered, inspected, snippet-only, and secondhand sources separately. Preserve contradictions and failed source recovery. Distinguish confidence, evidence state, and review state.

Every major conclusion requires a substantively independent source-seeking adversarial review, alternatives, falsification conditions, and a durable review record. A comparative claim about one named detection feature will not be adjudicated using generic proctoring descriptions alone.

## 12. Completion criteria

- Explain the product and buying workflow clearly before deriving lessons.
- Address every mandatory question with support or an explicitly reviewed, permitted evidence limitation.
- Trace material statements to source/claim records and complete independent review of major conclusions.
- Keep incorporation/launch, usage/payment, advertised/realized pricing, and reported/independently established outcomes distinct.
- Preserve consequential disputes and avoid treating budget exhaustion as evidence exhaustion.
- Deliver a connected, navigable book and criterion-by-criterion completion dossier with unresolved historical uncertainty visible.

Whether bounded public-evidence unknowns may count as an acceptable finished result awaits charter approval.

## 13. Exclusions

A current-growth strategy engagement would require revising these boundaries explicitly.
